# Training Component Library

Building a library for these movements requires a focus on **progressive loading** and **joint integrity**. Whether you are preparing for high-level calisthenics or simply improving mobility, these components target the "hinges" of the body—the wrists, spine, and shoulders.

---

## 1. Wrist & Spinal Mobility

These movements serve as the "pre-hab" foundation for the heavier pressing and pulling movements later in the list.

### Wrist Stretching
*   **Extensor:** Focus on the back of the forearm. Keep elbows locked to ensure the stretch hits the joint and not just the muscle belly.
*   **Flexor:** Palms down, fingers facing the knees. Lean back gently to increase the angle.

### Back Stretching
*   **Rolling like a Ball:** A spinal massage that encourages segmental control. Keep the chin tucked and the spine rounded.
*   **Plow:** A deep posterior chain stretch. Use this to decompress the cervical and thoracic spine after heavy bridges.


---

## 2. Bridge Progressions

Bridging transitions from simple glute activation to full spinal extension.

*   **Heel Elevated Glute:** Focuses on the posterior chain while minimizing quad involvement.
*   **Head Bridge:** The "intermediate" step. It builds neck strength and prepares the thoracic spine for the full arc.
*   **Hand Bridge (Full Bridge):** The goal is to stack the shoulders directly over the wrists, pushing the chest "through" the arms.


---

## 3. Inversion Foundations

Moving from a tripod base to a vertical line.

*   **Headstand (Fingered Tripod):** Unlike a standard tripod, using "fingered" tension improves micro-adjustments and finger strength, which translates directly to handstands.
*   **Crow:** The entry-point for arm balancing. It teaches "forward lean" and how to use the fingertips as brakes.
*   **Wall Walk:** The primary tool for building shoulder endurance. Walking the feet up the wall into a chest-to-wall handstand ensures a straight "hollow body" line.

---

## 4. Scapular Pull-Up Variations

These are not full pull-ups; they are **isolated scapular depressions**. They build the "active hang" necessary for one-arm work and heavy pulling.

| Variation | Focus Area |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Power Bottom (Ulnar)** | Emphasizes the outside edge of the palm for better torque. |
| **Commando/Neutral** | Easier on the shoulders; focuses on the mid-traps and rhomboids. |
| **Wide** | Increases the leverage disadvantage; targets the lower lats. |
| **Supinated (Underhand)** | More biceps involvement; prepares the elbows for the "back lever." |
| **Pronated (Overhand)** | The standard for overhead pulling strength. |
| **One-Arm** | The pinnacle of scapular stability. Requires intense core tension. |

---

## 5. Standing Leg Extensions

Focuses on the **active flexibility** of the hips and the strength of the glutes.

*   **Back/Backward Raise:** Keep the torso upright. Avoid leaning forward to "cheat" the height; focus on the glute squeeze.
*   **Kickback:** Often performed with a slight "pulse" at the top to engage the hamstrings and lower back.

> **Note on "Power Bottom" (Ulnar Grip):** This specific grip (pressing the pinky side of the hand into the bar) is excellent for golfers or gymnasts who need to strengthen the ulnar collateral ligament and prevent "elbow flare" during heavy pulls.
