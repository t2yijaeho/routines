# Training Component Library

---

## Aerobic & Recovery Components

### Slow Running (Recovery/Base)
*   **Zone 1:** This should feel like a "forever" pace. You should be able to breathe exclusively through your nose.
*   **Flat:** Minimizes eccentric loading on the joints, making it perfect for active recovery days.

### Fast Running (Threshold/Power)
*   **Zone 3:** This is "Comfortably Hard." Your breathing is labored, and conversation is limited to short sentences.
*   **Uphill:** Running on an incline reduces impact forces on the knees while significantly increasing the heart rate and posterior chain engagement.


### Power Walking (Aerobic Development)
*   **Zone 2:** The "sweet spot" for fat oxidation and mitochondrial health. You are moving with intent.
*   **Downhill:** This is excellent for **eccentric strengthening** of the quads and connective tissues around the patella. It mimics the "braking" force needed in sports.

### Slow Walking (Cool-Down/Lymphatic Drainage)
*   **Zone 1:** Purely for movement. 
*   **Flat:** Focus on a full heel-to-toe roll of the foot to stretch the plantar fascia after your handstand and calf-heavy work.

## Sample Session Structure: The "Sandwich" Protocol

Based on your requirement to start with **1 (Slow Run)**, repeat **2 (Fast Run)** and **3 (Power Walk)**, and finish with **4 (Slow Walk)**, here is how you should execute the session:

| Phase | Component | Duration/Intensity | Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Start** | **1. Slow Run** | 10–15 Minutes | Thermal warm-up; get blood to the extremities. |
| **Circuit** | **2. Fast Run (Uphill)** | 2–4 Minutes | Spike heart rate; build explosive leg power. |
| **Circuit** | **3. Power Walk (Downhill)** | 3–5 Minutes | Active recovery; eccentric quad loading. |
| **Repeat** | **Repeat 2 & 3** | 3–5 Rounds | Accumulate "Time under Tension" in Zone 3. |
| **Finish** | **4. Slow Walk** | 10 Minutes | Lower cortisol; return heart rate to baseline. |

### Integration Tip:
If you are combining this with your **Scapular Pull-ups** or **Handstand** work, perform the skill-heavy components (Handstands/Bridges) *after* the Slow Run but *before* the Fast Running intervals to ensure your nervous system isn't too fatigued for the balance work.
