# Training Component Library

## 1. Upper Body: Pull (Vertical & Horizontal)

Primary Focus: Latissimus dorsi, biceps, and grip strength.

### Pull Ups (Variations):

-Commando: A staggered grip where you pull your head to alternating sides of the bar. Increases lateral stability.
-Neutral: Palms facing each other. The most joint-friendly position for shoulders and elbows.
-Wide: A grip wider than shoulder-width to maximize recruitment of the outer lats.
-Supinated (Chin-up): Palms facing toward you, placing a higher mechanical load on the biceps.
-Pronated: The standard overhand grip; the foundation of vertical pulling.
-Alternate: One hand overhand, one hand underhand to manage grip fatigue.
-One Arm: The pinnacle of unilateral pulling strength and core tension.

## 2. Upper Body: Push

Primary Focus: Triceps, chest, and anterior deltoids.

### Dips:

- Bench: Hands on the edge of a bench with feet extended. Focuses on tricep isolation.
- Tricep: Performed on parallel bars with a vertical torso to keep tension on the arms.
- Chest: Performed with a forward lean and slightly flared elbows to engage the pectorals.

## 3. Lower Body: Leg Strength & Explosiveness

Primary Focus: Quads, glutes, hamstrings, and calves.

### Squats:

- Air: Basic bodyweight squat focusing on hip mobility and endurance.
- Bulgarian Split: One foot elevated behind you. Corrects imbalances and builds unilateral power.
- Jump: Adding an explosive vertical leap to develop fast-twitch muscle fibers.
- Shrimp: A single-leg squat holding the non-working foot behind the body.
- One Leg (Pistol): Full-depth single-leg squat with the other leg extended forward.

## 4. Cardiovascular & Conditioning

Primary Focus: Heart rate elevation and aerobic/anaerobic endurance.

### Running:

- Incline: Uphill grade training to increase posterior chain activation.
- Interval: Alternating high-intensity bursts with recovery periods.
- High Knee: Focuses on knee drive, hip flexor strength, and running mechanics.

## 5. Core: Flexion & Stability

Primary Focus: Rectus abdominis, obliques, and spinal stability.

### Hanging Leg Raises:

-Knee: Tucking knees to chest while hanging; accessible lower abdominal work.
-Toe to Bar: Full range of motion requiring high abdominal compression.

### Sit Ups:

- Decline: Performed on an angled bench to increase the resistance profile.
- Twisted: Incorporates rotation to target the internal and external obliques.

### Planks:

- Reverse: Facing upward, supported by hands and heels to strengthen the posterior chain.
